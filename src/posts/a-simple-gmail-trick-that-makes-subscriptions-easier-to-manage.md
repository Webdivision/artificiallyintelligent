Every once in a while, you come across a small digital trick that is simple, practical, and surprisingly useful. This Gmail tip felt like one of those.

The idea is straightforward. If you use Gmail, you can add #sub after your main address in a way that helps you identify certain signups or subscriptions more easily. It is one of those lightweight hacks that can make inbox organization a little less messy, especially if you are constantly signing up for newsletters, resources, tools, or trial offers.

What I like about tricks like this is not just the shortcut itself. It is the mindset behind it.

A lot of digital productivity comes down to small systems. Not giant overhauls. Not complicated automation. Just little adjustments that reduce friction and give you a bit more control over the flow of information.

Email is a perfect example.

Most people do not have an email problem because email is broken. They have an email problem because everything comes into the same place with very little structure. Over time, subscriptions, receipts, outreach, personal messages, client communication, and random one-off signups all get mixed together. That creates noise, and noise creates drag.

A trick like this gives you one more layer of control.

It lets you intentionally tag how you use your address in the wild. That can make it easier to filter incoming mail, identify where something came from, or simply keep certain categories of messages more visible and manageable.

For anyone who signs up for a lot of tools, content, or resources, that can be genuinely useful.

I also think there is a bigger lesson here. We tend to think productivity improvements have to come from major software upgrades or expensive systems. Sometimes they do. But sometimes the most effective improvements are simple changes that help you organize behavior better inside the tools you already use every day.

That is why I always like finding small workflow shortcuts like this.

They may not seem dramatic, but over time they can save attention, reduce clutter, and make day-to-day work feel cleaner.

Sometimes that is all you need.
