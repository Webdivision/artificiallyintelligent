I came across a video recently that made me think about logo design in a different way.

What stood out to me was not whether every brand should suddenly move into fully 3D logos. That is not really the point. The interesting part is what happens when a tool encourages you to think beyond the flat canvas and explore form, depth, movement, and space in a more open-ended way.

That kind of experimentation is valuable.

In branding, some of the best ideas do not start as polished final marks. They start as exploration. They come from looking at a concept from multiple angles, pushing it further than you originally planned, and discovering something unexpected in the process. Tools that make that kind of exploration easier can have real creative value, even if the final brand system ends up living mostly in two dimensions.

That is why I found this so interesting.

When you begin thinking in 3D, even loosely, it changes how you consider structure. You start noticing how forms relate to each other. You think more about volume, movement, tension, silhouette, and perspective. You begin to imagine how a symbol could behave, not just how it could sit statically on a page.

That can open up new territory.

Even if you ultimately flatten the idea back down, the process of exploring it in a more dimensional way can lead to stronger outcomes. It can help uncover more distinctive forms. It can expose weak ideas faster. It can push you out of default thinking and into something more original.

For me, that is the real appeal.

I like tools that invite curiosity. I like tools that make experimentation feel lighter and more accessible. When software helps creatives play with ideas rather than fight the interface, it tends to produce better work. Not because the software is doing the thinking for you, but because it is making room for better thinking to happen.

That is what I hope Adobe continues to push toward.

The best creative tools are not just efficient. They are inspiring. They make you want to test ideas, break patterns, and look at familiar problems in unfamiliar ways. That is often where the strongest work begins.

Not every experiment becomes a finished brand. But many strong brands start with experiments.

And that is why tools like this are worth paying attention to.
