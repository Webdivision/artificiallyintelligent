I've had a love affair with Adobe software for almost 30 years. Lately, though, I've been wondering what happened to the company I was so excited about.

I actually started in graphic arts before computers. Paste-up, mechanicals, picas, rulers, FPOs, the whole thing. When desktop publishing came along, I was hooked. What I loved was that the software spoke our language. It felt familiar, just with incredible new possibilities.

I remember finding a post on an early internet newsgroup about a training program for this new Adobe application called InDesign. At the time, QuarkXPress ruled the industry, and I had a serious love-hate relationship with it.

I convinced a friend to go with me, spent a few thousand dollars, and flew to Cupertino.

I'll never forget walking into that classroom at the old Apple campus. A sea of those colorful, translucent iMacs. We spent the course learning InDesign, and the best part? We got to take the iMacs home with the software installed!

InDesign was buggy as hell in those early days, but I could see where Adobe was going. The integration with Photoshop and Illustrator was incredible. It made sense to a designer.

I was sold.

I was also a huge fan of GoLive CyberStudio before Adobe acquired it. I loved the interface and how naturally it worked for graphic artists. When Adobe bought it, I thought, this is going to be great! Everything is going to work together.

Well, that didn't quite happen.

GoLive eventually disappeared, Dreamweaver took its place, and I started noticing decisions that felt more driven by business than by the needs of working creatives.

Still, I stuck with Adobe. I even defended the Creative Cloud subscription model when everyone was complaining about it. People forget what a nightmare software versioning used to be. You'd send a file to a printer and they couldn't open it because they were two versions behind. Subscriptions largely solved that problem, and to me, that was worth paying for.

But somewhere along the way, I feel like Adobe lost its way.

Now we're watching Figma, Canva, and a growing number of AI-powered tools challenge software that once seemed untouchable.

And honestly, I think Adobe fell asleep at the wheel a bit with AI. Some of the new features are interesting, but too many still feel like gimmicks rather than something that fundamentally improves how I work.

Meanwhile, I'm experimenting with tools that can do things I couldn't have imagined just a couple of years ago, sometimes for a fraction of the price.

That's a problem for Adobe.

I don't want to see them disappear. Quite the opposite. I'm rooting for them. I'd love to see them simplify their products, rethink their pricing, and get back to the kind of innovation that made me fall in love with their software in the first place.

Maybe it's time to stop adding features and start rethinking the experience.

The funny thing is, I remember when Adobe was the company challenging the industry standard.

Now they're the industry standard being challenged.

I really hope they remember what made them great.
