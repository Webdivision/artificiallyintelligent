I watched [this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4u94juYwLLM) and it connected with something I’ve been seeing firsthand.

For a long time, design followed a fairly familiar pattern. You got the brief, worked through concepts, built comps, revised, finalized, and pushed the files into production. That process still has value, and Adobe has been at the center of that world for years. I’ve spent a big part of my career working that way.

But the reality is, that model is starting to feel too rigid for how fast work moves now.

What I took from this video is not that process is dead. It’s that the old idea of a clean, linear design process is no longer enough on its own. Today, teams need to move faster, test sooner, collaborate more fluidly, and respond to changes in real time. That is especially true when you are dealing with high-volume production, constant revisions, multiple formats, and pressure from both branding and compliance.

That is a big part of why the shift away from a purely Adobe-based workflow matters.

Adobe tools are still powerful, and they still have a place. But when the process depends too heavily on building everything manually, too much time gets spent on production mechanics instead of thinking, direction, and decision-making. The work can become more about keeping up than actually improving the outcome.

What is more exciting to me now is the move toward a smarter process.

That means using tools that help teams explore ideas earlier, visualize direction faster, and make changes without restarting half the job. It means creating systems where design, strategy, and production are more connected. It means using AI where it actually helps, not to replace design thinking, but to remove friction and give experienced creatives more room to do what they do best.

That is what a modern workflow should do.

It should help you get to clarity faster. It should make feedback easier to apply. It should let teams work more collaboratively and reduce the amount of repetitive production work that slows everything down. And it should make it easier to adapt when messaging, offers, legal copy, or priorities shift, because they always do.

From my perspective, this is the real change. It is not just about moving from Adobe to something newer. It is about moving from a tool-centered workflow to a smarter process-centered one.

That is a very different mindset.

The goal is no longer just to make the file. The goal is to build a workflow that helps people think better, communicate more clearly, and produce stronger work with less waste.

That is why this video stood out to me.

It reflects something a lot of creative teams are going to have to face. The old process is not completely gone, but it is no longer enough by itself. The future is going to belong to teams that can combine strong creative judgment with smarter systems, faster iteration, and more flexible tools.

And honestly, I think that is a good thing.
